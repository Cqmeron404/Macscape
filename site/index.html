Drift

Weather for your desktop that never gets in your way.

Snow, rain, petals and fireflies settle on your windows, widgets and desktop icons. They fall behind everything, so nothing you're working on is ever covered.

Version 1.0 Size 1.5 MB Needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later Runs on Apple silicon and Intel
Setup · about two minutes

Install it in four steps

Drift was made by a friend, not downloaded from the App Store, so macOS asks you to confirm it once the first time you open it. After that it opens like any other app.

1

Download and move it

Click Download Drift.zip above. Open your Downloads folder and double-click the zip if Safari didn't unzip it for you. Drag Drift into Applications.

2

Open it once and confirm

  1. Double-click Drift. macOS says it can't verify the app. Click Done.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll to the bottom.
  3. Next to "Drift was blocked", click Open Anyway, then enter your password.
Prefer Terminal? One command does it
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Drift.app && open /Applications/Drift.app
3

Pick your weather

Click the weather icon in the Mac menu bar to choose an effect and adjust its intensity.

4

Let weather land on desktop icons

For this optional effect, choose Solid Desktop Icons in the menu and allow Drift in System Settings under Accessibility. Drift uses this permission to locate desktop icons.

Nine kinds of weather

Switch any time from the menu bar

Snow

Piles up softly on window tops and the Dock. Hold ⌥ Option over it to pack a snowball, then throw it.

Rain

Splashes where it lands. Turn on flood mode and the water slowly rises behind your windows.

Thunderstorm

Heavy rain and branching lightning that strikes the tops of your windows in a burst of sparks.

Tornado

Wind-blown leaves, twigs and paper, and now and then a funnel that sweeps across and pulls it all up.

Sakura

Cherry petals drift down and rest on whatever they reach.

Autumn Leaves

Leaves collect on your windows. Sweep the pointer across a pile to scatter it.

Fireflies

Soft lights wander the desktop and gather wherever your pointer rests.

Starfall

Meteors streak across and break into sparks when they hit something.

Dust Storm

Gusts of sand that pile up on your windows, a hazy sky, and the odd tumbleweed.

Questions

Good to know

How do I build a snowman?

With Snow on, hold Option with the pointer over fallen snow. A snowball packs and grows as you move along the pile. Let go of Option over another ball to stack it. Three in a stack become a snowman.

What happens when I throw one?

Move the pointer and let go of Option to throw. A soft landing settles. A hard hit on a window, widget or the edge of the screen bursts it into snow that piles up. Snowmen can be picked up and thrown too.

Can I change the wind?

Yes. Use the wind strength and angle sliders in the Drift menu. The angle tilts everything that falls, from rain to petals.

Does the flood ever stop?

It rises until the screen is full. Choose Drain the Water in the menu, switch flood mode off, or change the weather and it drains away.

Will it cover my windows?

No. Drift draws just above your wallpaper and below every window, widget and icon. Clicks always go straight through.

Does it slow my Mac down?

It's light. It runs at half rate in Low Power Mode and pauses when a full-screen app covers the display.

Why does macOS warn me about it?

Apps from the App Store or from registered developers are checked by Apple first. Drift was built by a friend and shared directly, so you confirm it yourself once.

How do I remove it?

Click the Drift icon in the menu bar, choose Quit Drift, then drag Drift from Applications to the Trash.
